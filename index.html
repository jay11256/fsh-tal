Automated Temporal Localization of Behavior in Cichlid Fish with Few‑Shot Trajectory Tokens

Bhargav Srinivasan1,2,*, William Lamousin1,*, Jason Liu1,†, Charles Phan1,†, Ofure Osunbor2, Shubh Sharma1, Cade McGeehan2, Tahir Haroon2, Vijay Jayasuriya1, Ariel Bazan1, Coltan G. Parker2, Scott Juntti2, Pulkit Kumar1, Abhinav Shrivastava1

The problem

Behavioral experiments are limited by how fast experts can watch video.

  • Experts annotate at real-time playback, hour by hour, in BORIS.
  • Four observers per recording, reconciled by inter-rater reliability.
  • Each label is a single time point. No start, no end.
17
hour-long recordings
8,202
expert point annotations
67.7%
fall within 2 s of another annotation

The dataset

Six short, visually similar, often overlapping behaviors.

17 hour-long recordings of Astatotilapia burtoni pairs, side view, 25 fps.

Tracking · object-grounded trajectories

Segmentation based keypoint tracking.

SAM3 segments both fish in every frame, and points are tracked across the masks to give each fish a trajectory.

No skeleton or keypoint labels provided.

The model · FSH-TAL

A few-shot classifier feeds a point-supervised localizer.

Few-shot MIL classifier
Trajectory-aligned tokens at the tracked points fused with motion features, trained in few-shot episodes.
Point-supervised localizer
Behavior, timespan, and centerness heads, trained on ±2 s pseudo-segments around each point annotation.

Results

State of the art at every IoU threshold over varied supervision.

Average mAP, IoU 0.1–0.7

Per-behavior average precision

Well-defined behaviors localize best, even when they are rare.

Precisely defined behaviors beat loosely defined ones: Tilt and Peck are rare, yet localize best.

FSH-TAL beats every baseline on all six behaviors.

IoU 0.1–0.7, over the recordings that contain each behavior.
