TL;DRPoint-supervised temporal action localization for cichlid behavior, trained only on point annotations. collect.

Experts currently annotate cichlid courtship behavior as single timestamped points, with no record of how long each behavior lasted. FSH-TAL turns those sparse points into classified, temporally localized segments over hour-long aquarium recordings, reaching 42.8% mAP (IoU 0.1–0.7).

Abstract

Understanding the brain requires studying its observable output: behavior. Cichlid fish provide a model system for changing brain function and studying its effect on well-defined behaviors. Such experiments require manual annotation of aquarium video at real-time playback speed, which is time consuming to generate and produces single time points that lack the temporal context of the underlying behavior. We provide a benchmarking dataset for animal behavior containing ∼17 hours of annotated cichlid footage, and propose FSH-TAL, a temporal localization model that learns from point annotations and predicts behavior-specific temporal segments in full-length videos. On our dataset, FSH-TAL reaches state-of-the-art mAP (42.8% averaged across IoU 0.1–0.7).

Method


FSH-TAL has three stages.
(A) SAM3 segments both fish from the text prompt “fish” and we track 18 points per clip across the predicted masks.
(B) A few-shot MIL classifier, built on Trokens, fuses DINOv2 features with the tracked trajectories, trained in 5-way 3-shot episodes so co-occurring behaviors share a clip.
(C) FSH-TAL trains a temporal transformer on the frozen MIL embeddings at 4 FPS, predicting behavior, timespan, and centerness, decoded with greedy NMS per behavior.

17 hour-long recordings of paired Astatotilapia burtoni (25 FPS) give 8,202 point-annotated events across six behaviors: Bite, Lead, Peck, Quiver, Chase, and Tilt, each annotated by 4 observers for inter-rater reliability.
